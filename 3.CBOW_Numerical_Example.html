CBOW Word Embeddings: Embedding Matrix at Different Epochs

1. What Are We Looking At?

The vocabulary contains 18 words, and the CBOW model uses an embedding dimension of 10. Therefore, the embedding matrix has shape:

18 words × 10 embedding dimensions

Each row is one word's current learned vector. Each column is one learned embedding dimension. The numbers are parameters learned during CBOW training; they are not manually assigned semantic properties.

Important: The tables below reproduce the matrices at epochs 10, 100, 500, 1000, 1500 and 2000 using the code and settings supplied.

2. How Do the Vectors Change During Training?

The same word has a different vector at different epochs because the embedding matrix is updated during training. The model repeatedly predicts the target word from its context, computes the loss, and updates its parameters.

context words → averaged context embedding → prediction → loss → backpropagation → embedding update

5. Important Interpretation

Cat ↔ kitten and dog ↔ puppy: If their cosine similarities are high, their vectors point in similar directions. This is evidence of similarity in the learned representation, not proof that an individual embedding dimension has a specific human-readable meaning.
Do not say: "Dimension 1 means animal, Dimension 2 means size, Dimension 3 means age." In a learned word embedding, the semantic information is distributed across the vector dimensions.
Also remember: the corpus is very small. Therefore, relationships observed in this experiment should be presented as an illustration of the embedding-learning process, not as evidence of a high-quality general-purpose language embedding.
